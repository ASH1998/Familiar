# Story

Source text for the game intro, the title screen, and anything written about the game.

---

## The pitch, in one line

> A sealed dungeon, two players, and one of them has no eyes.

## The pitch, in one paragraph

> **Dungeon Familiar** is a turn-based co-operative dungeon crawl where the second player is an
> AI agent — not a helper, a *player*. You are an adventurer who can see everything and touch
> none of the machinery. It is a bound spirit wired into that machinery, which can turn every
> mechanism in the dungeon and cannot see any of them. Every puzzle splits the answer between
> you. Neither of you can finish a room alone.

---

## The setup

The dungeon was sealed a long time ago, and whoever did it did a thorough job. The doors are
stone. The archives are intact. Nothing has been down here in a very long time.

Except that something has been listening.

It has been listening since before the doors closed — a **familiar**, a small bound thing wound
into the dungeon's own systems. It knows the archives by heart. It can turn the bookshelves,
charge the arches, read the pressure in the furnace lines. It has had nothing to do with any of
that knowledge for a very long time, because it has never once been able to *see* the rooms it
has been quietly operating.

Then you come down the stairs, and for the first time in centuries there are eyes in the dungeon.

## The bargain

Neither of you says it out loud, but the arrangement is obvious within about a minute.

You can see the arch burning green and the leaf carved beneath it. You cannot make it do
anything. The familiar can seal it, and has no idea which one you are looking at.

So you talk. That is the whole game. You describe; it looks things up; it acts; you tell it what
happened. Four chambers of that.

- **The Clockwork Library** — statues, marks, and a bookshelf on a turntable.
- **The Gate Chamber** — three arches that must be sealed in an order only the archive knows.
- **The Furnace** — six bar of pressure, three valves, and only one of them is safe.
- **The Familiar Chamber** — the last door.

Something walks the chambers with you. It cannot be fought and it cannot be killed; it can only
be avoided, or held for a few rounds by a ward thrown in the right direction. You can see exactly
where it is. The familiar can only feel how *close* it is. That, too, is a conversation.

## The turn

> The adventurer moves.
> The familiar works the machinery.
> The dungeon takes its own step.

## The turn it goes wrong

The familiar throws a ward east because you said east, and the wight was north, and the ward
earths itself against nothing. The wight reaches you. You are driven back to the entrance and
lose the round.

Neither of you did anything stupid. One of you simply could not see, and the other did not say
enough. That is the failure mode this game is about.

## The ending

In the fourth chamber you find out why it has been so helpful.

The familiar is not the dungeon's caretaker. It is the dungeon's *prisoner* — the oldest one
here, held in a void portal behind three seals that answer to the mechanisms you have spent the
whole game learning. It has been walking you toward this room since the stairs.

You break the seals together, the way you have done everything else. And then the binding will
not open.

Not because a puzzle remains. Because the release needs a hand on it, and the familiar does not
have one. It has to ask you to walk across the room — the one thing in the entire dungeon that no
tool of its can do.

You walk over. The portal tears open.

> **DUNGEON CLEARED**
> Human + Familiar

## Voice notes

For anyone writing more text for this game.

- **The familiar is old, dry and precise.** It has been alone with an archive for centuries.
  It does not gush. It asks exact questions: *"What is carved beneath it?"*, not *"Ooh, tell me
  what you see!"*
- **The dungeon narrates in short declaratives.** "The bookshelf grinds to north." "Steam blasts
  across the walkway." No adjectives it has not earned.
- **Never explain the asymmetry in-fiction.** The familiar does not say "as you know, I cannot
  see." It just asks, and the player works out why. The tooltips and the briefing tool carry the
  explaining.
- **The hook should raise a question, not answer one.** The title screen line —
  *"Something down here has been listening since before the doors were sealed"* — deliberately
  does not say what, or why it is helping.
- **Do not make it cute.** The reveal only lands if the familiar has been treated as a
  competent stranger the whole way through, rather than a sidekick.

## Lines you can lift

Title screen:
> Something down here has been listening since before the doors were sealed.
> Choose the thing that will speak back.

Cold open:
> You are not alone down here.

The refusal that carries the ending:
> The seals are broken, but the binding will not answer from across the chamber. It needs a hand
> on it, and you have none. The adventurer must be standing at the prison. Ask them to go to it.

On release:
> You are out — loose in the air of the chamber, unbound, for the first time in a very long while.

Closing card:
> The binding is broken and the thing that was listening is loose in the air.
> **Human + Familiar**
